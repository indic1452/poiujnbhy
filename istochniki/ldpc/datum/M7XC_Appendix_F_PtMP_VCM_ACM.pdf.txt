Point to Multi-Point VCM / ACM Operation Overview and Theory 
In a Point to Multi-Point network environment a common operational configuration is to 
configure the “outbound traffic” (from Hub site to Remote Sites) as a single broadcast 
carrier from hub to satellite and satellite to remote.  Traffic that is destined to a particular 
site or broadcast and multi-cast traffic destined to multiple sites are decoded and 
ingested by the appropriate site(s). 
 
Returning traffic (often called inbound traffic) is generated at the remote site location 
and sent back to the Hub.  This return traffic can be via FDMA SCPC carrier, Dynamic 
Carrier Assignment or co-exist on shared carriers in the time domain such as TDMA.  
For the purposes of this Appendix, we will focus on FDMA return carriers.

An outbound Hub carrier that is received by multiple remote sites at varying quality 
levels historically meant that all of the outbound user traffic had to be “protected” with a 
single, fixed modulation and coding scheme (MODCOD) that ensured the most 
disadvantaged site could demodulate and decode the signal as shown in the below 
example (Figure 3-1).  
 
Site 1 has the lowest perceived signal quality due being a mobile site with a small 
antenna.  The demodulator Es/No reading of 2.5dB is the weakest in the example 
network. Historical networks would limit all of the Hub Outbound traffic to a MODCOD 
that could be locked to signal of 2.5dB or lower without errors to guarantee link 
availability to all sites. 
 
 
Figure 3-1 VCM/ACM with Various Remote Es/No 
 
A more modern method to deal with varied remote site signal quality is to 
compartmentalize the Outbound user traffic into bursts that are encapsulated with 
varying levels of protection.  The method is commonly referred to as ACM (Adaptive 
Coding and Modulation).  In this way the outbound aggregate traffic is packaged with 
multiple levels of protection such that sites that benefit from a higher signal quality can 
receive higher MODCODs when their signal is strong and maximize user traffic while 
other sites will use more robust signal protection at the expense of lower user 
throughput for the same BW used.  Datum offers a dynamic outbound ACM operation 
where each site is dynamically sent data in the best fit MODCOD rather than a fixed 
MODCOD for the site or the network.

Configuring VCM/ACM Operation on M7XC modems 
 
Now that we understand the theory of Outbound VCM operation and the benefits of 
maximizing the satellite efficiency on a per site basis we can configure the modems to 
function in a VCM environment. 
 
For the purposes of this manual, we will associate the following names to the various 
modems in the VCM/ACM Network: 
 
Hub TX/RX Modem:  The M7XC configured as the Tx modem at the Hub site that is 
responsible for transmitting the shared Tx outbound carrier and multi-demod landing 
up to 16 sites (Flex LDPC) or 2 sites (DVB-S2X). 
 
Hub RX-Only Demod: One or more M7XC modems configured as a demod or multi-
demod ONLY.  This Rx-Only demod is capable of landing up to sixteen (16) Flex 
LDPC sites, or two (2) DVB-S2X sites, or one (1) site running DSSS.  Note: Hub Rx-
Only Demods are ONLY needed if the VCM/ACM network requires more sites that the 
Hub RX/RX Modem is capable of.  The TOTAL number of sites that the VCM/ACM 
network can have can not exceed 16 sites.  
 
Remote Modem:  A remote site in a VCM/ACM network

 Outbound VCM/ACM VLAN Tags 
VCM works in conjunction with VLAN 802.1Q tagging.  Ideally, user traffic at the Hub 
site that is destined for the remote sites should be pre-identified and pre-tagged by 
an external router / switch or 3rd party device.  Associating a subnet or set of IP / 
MAC address to a VLAN ID of a remote site is outside of the scope of this Appendix and 
should be discussed with the proper networking staff.  Using one mechanism or 
another, any traffic that is strictly destined for a specific remote site should be 
encapsulated with the outermost VLAN tag unique to that remote site.  VLAN tags for 
broadcast or multicast traffic can also be configured by the networking staff. 
 
Once the Hub to Remote user traffic is properly tagged the hub modem can be 
configured to perform a lookup to associate a VLAN tag or VLAN range to a VCM 
selection and therefore a MODCOD level of protection.

• Queue Paired RX Channel:  This is the channel identifier that will be embedded 
in the sent message, a remote site with the matching Queue Paired RX Channel 
identifier will be allowed to demodulate and decode this user traffic. 
• Queue CIR Mode: Normal is the nominal setting.  A setting of Periodic will allow

• Queue Min Es/No: User selected between -5.0 and 1.99dB.  This setting will 
essentially give a floor to the lowest MODCOD to be used for the Queue in cases 
or poor weather or link outage.  The lower the value allowed the more robust the 
link will stay up but the lower the user throughput. A user may want to set this the 
minimum to allow the link to stay up as long as possible or may want to set it to a 
higher level such as 1 or 2 so that VERY low MODCODs are not used to try and 
attain the “Desired Queue CIR”.  A low MODCOD combined with an excessive 
Desired CIR can result in too many frames being used to attain one Queue’s CIR 
and thus penalizing the entire network. 
• Queue Max Es/No: User selected between -4.99 and 25dB.  This setting will 
essentially give a ceiling to the maximum MODCOD to be used for the Queue.  A 
ceiling MODCOD may be desirable to limit the user traffic to a particular site or to 
prevent higher order MODCODs such as 64APSk and > from running if the link is 
not highly linear. 
• Desired Queue CIR:  This is the minimum user Datarate (committed information 
rate) that is desired for use by the currently selected Queue.  The Hub modulator 
will attempt to give more or less outbound DVB-S2X frames to satisfy this 
Queue’s Desired CIR.  A low MODCOD setting or a low Queue Min Es/No may 
require a substantial percentage of the hub TX carrier to meet high Desired CIR 
figures when running at low MODCODs.

The following settings impact all Queues at the Hub TX/RX Modulator: 
 
• Desired Drain Time:  User selectable from 100 – 500ms. This is the target 
maximum time for a packet placed into a Queue to wait to drain to the WAN.  
Lower settings can be used to show improvement in jitter while higher settings 
can be used to increase efficiency 
• Drain Time @ Min ModCods:  Read only status as an indicator to the operator 
that IF all queues met their Queue Min Es/No at the same time, it would take 
this much time to drain all queues and meet the combined Total Desired CIR.  If 
this number is substantially greater than your “Desired Drain Time” you may want 
to consider increasing the Min Es/No per Queue, reducing your Desired Queue 
CIR,  or increase the overall capacity of your TX link. 
• Total Desired CIR:  The addition of all Queue(s) Desired Queue CIR plus some 
modem overhead 
• Total CIR @ Min ModCods: This is the maximum user throughput of the total 
system if all sites were running at their lowest MODCOD. 
• Queue Live Time:  The maximum amount of time an Ethernet frame can stay in a 
queue without being transmitted due to drain congestion.  After this time the 
packet will be discarded.

The TBERD 6000A is the Hub network.  M7XC #1 transmits the outbound shared 
carrier to all four remotes.   M7XC #1 also receives the return channels from remotes #1 
and #2.   M7XC receives the return channels from remotes #3 and #4.

M7XC#1 maps VLAN 101 to remote #1, VLAN 102 to remote #2, VLAN 103 to remote 
#3 and VLAN 104 to remote #4. 
The traffic between the TBERD and switch port 1 (P1) is a VLAN trunk carrying traffic 
for all remotes in VLANs 101, 102, 103, 104.   The traffic between switch port 2 (P2) 
and M7XC #1 carries transmit traffic for all remotes in VLANs 101, 102, 103 ,104, and 
carries receive traffic from remote #1 and remote #2 in VLANs 101, 102.   The traffic 
between switch port 3 (P3) and M7XC #2 carries transmit traffic in VLANs 103, 104 
which is dropped by M7XC #2, and carries receive traffic from remote #3 and remote #4 
in VLANs 103 and 104. 
Untagged traffic must go between P2 and P3 to allow ACM Es/N0 packets from P3 to 
P2 and the ACK for those packets from P3 to P2. 
An unmanaged switch cannot be used, and return traffic from VLANs 103 and 104 is 
learned at P3, and the switch will then send transmit traffic for VLANs 103 and 104 to 
P3 instead of P2. 
The solution is to apply the appropriate VLANs to P1, P2 and P3, and mirror the 
transmit traffic from P3 to P2.  When mirroring the transmit traffic from P3 to P2, P2 
should have "Network Traffic" = "Enabled" (Cisco) or "Ingress State" = "Enable" 
(Planet). 
Setup: 
1. VCM TRAFFIC 
1.1. Create VLANs 101, 102, 103 & 104. 
1.2. Assign VLAN 101, 102, 103 & 104 to P1 (tagged). 
1.3. Assign VLAN 101 & 102 to P2 (tagged). 
1.4. Assign VLAN 103 & 104 to P3 (tagged) 
1.5. Mirror P3 Tx (Egress) to P2 with Traffic=Enabled or Ingress State=Enable. 
2. ACM TRAFFIC (optional, this will prevent ACM traffic from hitting the TBERD) 
2.1. Create VLAN 2 
2.2. Assign VLAN 2 to P2 (untagged). 
2.3. Assign VLAN 2 to P3 (untagged).
