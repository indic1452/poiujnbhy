1.0   SCOPE 
 
          This document provides the generic characteristics of Intermediate Data Rate (IDR) 
equipment  for operating with all types of earth stations working in INSAT system 
using modulation switchable between QPSK and TCM-8PSK with options for  
information rate as N*64 kbps (where N=1 to 32), 2.048 Mbps,  8.448 Mbps and 
34.368 Mbps. The design of IDR carriers shall be based upon the ITU-R 
Recommendations S.522-5, S.614-4 and S.579-6. These carriers may share 
transponders with carriers employing other approved modulation techniques and/or 
with other IDR carriers. The occupied satellite bandwidth unit for these carriers is 
equal to 0.7(QPSK)/0.467(TCM-8 PSK) times the transmission rate inclusive of guard 
band between adjacent carriers. The specifications of Up/Down converters  forming 
part of the IDR Equipment  have also been provided. The system shall be configured 
with redundancy of (1:1) for remote station and (N:1) for main station where N can be 
upto 7. 
 
1.1  FUNCTIONAL AND TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS 
 
1.1.1     FUNCTIONAL  REQUIREMENTS 
 
1.1.1.1  IDR equipment shall be designed to comply with ITU-R Recommendations S.522-5, 
S.614-4   and S.579-6.  
 
1.1.1.2  The IDR Equipment shall support the concatenation of Reed-Solomon (RS) code. 
The shortened systematic Reed-Solomon Codes are listed in Table-1.  
 
1.1.1.3  Since errors from the decoder  tend to occur in bursts, block interleaving shall be 
employed in conjunction with the RS outercode to reduce the correlation among  
erroneous symbols in a RS Code word. The interleaving details  shall be as per IESS 
308 Rev.11 and IESS 310 Rev.3 documents. 
 
1.1.1.4 For IDR Carrier with rate 2 Mb/s, the RS Encoder/ Interleaver shall be inserted after  
the 96 kb/s ESC overhead framing unit and before the FEC encoder. On the receive 
side (demodulator) the RS decoder/de-interleaver shall be inserted after the FEC 
decoder and before the 96 kb/s ESC overhead deframing unit. 
 
1.1.1.5  It shall be possible to enable or disable the use of RS outer coding from the front-
panel control of the IDR channel unit. 
 
1.1.1.6  Since the transmission rate and FEC used for IDR modulator and demodulator may 
be  different for the transmit (modulator) and receive (demodulator) paths, it shall be 
possible to independently select the same or different RS codecs for RS encoder and 
decoder respectively  in  order to be compatible with the transmission rates (hence 
RS code words) being used by the IDR modulator and demodulator, irrespective of 
the inner FEC rate ( 1/2 or 3/4  with QPSK modulation and 2/3 with TCM-8PSK 
modulation) being used. 
 
1.1.1.7   Programmability of the RS Codec shall be provided to allow maximum flexibility 
and ease of use in selecting the different RS Codes corresponding to the various IDR 
information rates. 

=====PAGE 7=====
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1.1.1.8  Synchronization and scrambling requirements shall be as per IESS 308 Rev.11 and 
IESS 310 Rev 3. 
 
1.1.1.9  When using the RS outer  coding, the following shall be provided: 
  
a)  An indication of the composite rate (i.e., information rate plus overhead) Eb/No at 
the input  to the RS decoder. The Eb/No reading shall be derived from the inner 
FEC modem and shall have an accuracy of equal to or better than 0.5dB   over a 
range extending from the point at which the inner FEC modem loses 
synchronization to a maximum value of at least 10 dB. Design objectives are:  an 
Eb/No measurement accuracy of  0.25 dB (or  better) for Eb/No values less than 
or equal to 10 dB and a maximum Eb/No readout of 15dB (±1 dB accuracy, for 
10dB<Eb/No 15dB). 
 
              b) A "low Eb/No" alarm to signify the onset of degraded RS decoder performance. 
The activation of this alarm shall only trigger appropriate audible and visual 
indicators. It shall be  possible to adjust the trigger level for this alarm over an 
Eb/No range of 5 dB to 7 dB in incremental steps of 0.25 dB. 
 
          c)  The BER at the input to the RS decoder shall be displayed over a range of about 
10-2 to at least 10-9. 
 
          d)  The BER at the output to the RS decoder shall be displayed over a range of 10-4 to 
at least 10-10. This BER value shall be derived from the input BER versus output 
BER performance characteristic of the RS decoder (e.g., by using a look-up table 
of the transfer characteristic).

2.0    GENERAL REQUIREMENTS  
 
2.1     Engineering requirements 
2.1.1 The joints in the equipment shall have protection as per BIS standard IS 12063 {1987}” 
Classification of degree of protection provided by enclosures of the electrical 
equipment” {equivalent to IEC-529} to meet at least the protection level of IP-65. 
 
2.1.2 The equipment should follow proper construction practice to minimise unintended 
radiations due to leakage from any gap or monitoring points. All unused if/rf ports 
and monitoring points should be terminated. The power flux density shall not exceed 
1 mW/cm2 at a distance of 2.5 cms. 
2.1.3   The equipment shall adopt state-of-the-art technology.  
2.1.4   The equipment shall be of compact and composite construction and lightweight.  The 
actual dimensions and weight shall be furnished by the manufacturer. 
2.1.5  All connectors shall be reliable and of standard type to ensure for over 500 failure-free 
mating operations under the environmental conditions specified. 
 
2.1.6   All connectors and the cable used shall be of low loss type and suitably shielded. 
 
2.1.7   The equipment shall be housed in standard 19” ( 600 mm) rack or slim rack with a 
maximum height of 2250 mm and with front access. For outdoor equipment (or part of 
equipment), construction practice shall be followed as specified. 
 
2.1.8  The equipment shall preferably work on natural cooling. However, the use of in-built DC 
operated fans is permitted in some equipment. An alarm shall be extended to indicate 
failure of fan/fans. MTBF of each fan shall be more than 50,000 hours. 
 
2.1.9 
The supervisory indications and other control switches shall be provided at convenient 
locations on the bay preferably at a height around 1500 mm for ease of maintenance. 
 
2.1.10 
The plug-in units shall be hot swappable to allow their removal/insertion while the 
equipment is in energized condition. 
 
2.1.11 
The mechanical design and construction of each/unit shall be inherently robust and rigid 
under all conditions of operation, adjustment, replacement, storage and transport and 
conforming to para 12 of BSNL QA document No. QM-333 {latest issue} “Specification 
for environmental testing of electronic equipments for transmission and switching use”. 
 
2.1.12 
Each sub-assembly shall be clearly marked with schematic reference to show its function, 
so that it is identifiable from the layout diagram in the handbook. 
 
2.1.13 
Each terminal block and individual tags shall be numbered suitably with clear identifying 
code and shall correspond to the associated wiring drawings. 
 
2.1.14 
All controls, switches, indicators etc. shall be clearly marked to show their circuit 
designation and functions. 
 

=====PAGE 20=====
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2.1.15   Important Do’s and Don’ts about the operation of the equipment shall be clearly 
indicated at a convenient place on the equipment.

OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS

            without any degradation in performance up to at an altitude upto 3,600 meters.

saline atmosphere near coastal areas and should be protected against corrosion.

and red colour for unhealthy conditions may be provided. Some other colour may be used

            date and time caused by events such as changeover leap year etc. in the normal

cooling arrangements, input/output, isolation /monitoring ports, as required.
