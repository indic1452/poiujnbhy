Abstract. 
For those n < 5000 for which the factorization of 2" - 1 is known,
the first primitive trinomial (if such exists) and a randomly generated primitive
5- and 7-nomial of degree n in GF(2) are given.
A primitive polynomial of degree n over GF(2) is useful for generating a
pseudorandom sequence of «-tuples of zeros and ones, see [8]. If the polyno-
mial has a small number k of terms, then the sequence is easily computed. But
for cryptological applications (correlation attack, see [5]) it is often necessary to
have the primitive polynomials with larger values of k than one can find in the
existing tables. For example, Zierler and Brillhart [10, 11] have calculated all
irreducible trinomials of degree n < 1000, with the period for some for which
the factorization of 2" - 1 is known; Stahnke [7] has listed one example of a
trinomial or pentanomial of degree n < 168 ; Zierler [12] has listed all primitive
trinomials whose degree is a Mersenne exponent < 11213 = A/23 (there, M¡
denotes the jfh Mersenne exponent); Rodemich and Rumsey [6] have listed
all primitive trinomials of degree M¡■, 12 < j < 17 ; Kurita and Matsumoto
[2] have listed all primitive trinomials of degree M¡, 24 < j < 28, and one
example of primitive pentanomials of degree M¡, 8 < j < 27.

The 5- and 7-nomials listed in Table 1 were obtained using a random number
generator. Randomly chosen /c-nomials of degree n are checked for primitivity

The primitivity check is carried out using the factorizations of 2" - 1 from
